# Boiling and still-camera speckles: cause and fix (2026-11-19/20)

The denoised image "boiled" (bright blotches crawling over walls and objects), and on
the Steam Deck a camera held still grew small, static fireflies 2-10 s after stopping.
Both have one cause in the renderer, not in the networks. All numbers are measured
unless marked otherwise.

## Cause: the 1-spp frames repeat their error

`training/temporal/correlation.py` on raw frames from a still camera (480x270, 256
frames, log(1 + exposure x luminance)). "Left after 16" is the noise remaining in the
mean of 16 consecutive frames; independent frames would leave a quarter.

| scene, renderer | noise per pixel | same 1 / 16 frames later | left after 16 frames |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| living room, plain path tracing | 0.182 | 0% / 0% | 0.046 (a quarter) |
| living room, ReSTIR DI + GI | 0.057 | 88% / 62% | 0.053 (93%) |
| day Bistro, ReSTIR DI + GI | 0.043 | 49% / 34% | 0.031 (71%) |
| Sponza, ReSTIR DI + GI | 0.038 | 10% / 7% | 0.015 (39%) |
| night Bistro, ReSTIR DI only | 0.029 | 9% / 1% | 0.008 (29%) |
| night Bistro, ReSTIR DI + GI | 0.022 | 28% / 10% | 0.010 (47%) |

- The repetition comes from ReSTIR GI. A pixel's reservoir shows one bounce sample
  until another replaces it: with the history capped at 20 samples and one fresh sample
  per pixel every 4 frames (2x2 blocks), about 80 frames. A bright sample's weight
  decays by 20/21 per new sample, so one 1000 times the average stays visible for
  seconds.
- Lamp reuse (ReSTIR DI) is nearly independent from frame to frame: its spatial merge
  picks among seven reservoirs anew every frame.
- Tracing the bounce at every pixel (4x the rays) does not help: the cap, not the
  sample rate, sets how much a reservoir knows (living room, 8x8 blocks, left after 16
  frames: 0.0098 with blocks, 0.0095 with every pixel).
- Consequences: a temporal filter cannot average the error away, the denoiser shows
  standing bright pixels as blobs that move when reservoirs change, and the still-view
  average (`temporal.rs`: the display fades from the denoised frame to the mean of the
  raw frames) keeps them, which is the speckle that appears seconds after stopping.

A measurement trap found on the way: with the tuner's visibility raster or with
upscaling, all pixels share one sub-pixel jitter per frame (Halton 2, 3), and texture
detail then alternates with periods 2 and 3. `correlation.py` therefore needs
`--center-jitter --no-auto-tune`.

## Fix: shading reuse (`raytracer.wgsl`, `GI_SHADE`)

What a pixel shows is resampled every frame from the reservoirs of 3 random pixels
within 24 pixels on the same surface (last frame's reservoirs around where the pixel
was; same tangent plane, normal within 26 degrees, reconnection Jacobian within 0.1-10),
followed by one visibility ray. The pixel's own sample is shown only when no neighbour
qualifies or the pick is hidden, and then at no more than 3 times what the neighbours
found. The stored history is untouched. While the view is still, the history cap grows
with the samples traced (`giCap`), so reservoirs keep improving instead of forgetting.

Still camera, raw frames:

| scene | same 1 frame later | left after 16 frames, per pixel | per 8x8 block | brightness of a long average |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| living room, before | 88% | 0.053 | 0.0098 | 1.00 |
| living room, now | 5% | 0.020 (-62%) | 0.0061 (-38%) | 0.97 |
| day Bistro, before | 49% | 0.031 | 0.0066 | 1.00 |
| day Bistro, now | 3% | 0.016 (-48%) | 0.0062 (-6%) | 0.99 |
| night Bistro, before | 28% | 0.010 | 0.0052 | not measured |
| night Bistro, now | 16% | 0.008 (-26%) | 0.0050 | not measured |

Through the app's own pipeline (960x540, half resolution, v4 `td-half16`, TAAU):
yesterday's renderer (commit 2a9aa55) against today's, with everything on this page.
Still: 300 frames, flicker over the second half, PSNR against an 8192-spp reference.
Moving: 200 frames at 0.08 degrees and half a centimetre per frame.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| living room | 0.0037 -> 0.0014 | 25.26 -> 26.66 dB | 0.950 -> 0.939 | 0.0020 -> 0.0019 |
| day Bistro | 0.0049 -> 0.0034 | 26.43 -> 27.57 dB | 0.951 -> 0.989 | 0.0054 -> 0.0059 |

Night Bistro's numbers are set by its lamps (1% of the pixels hold 78% of the light, and
the display adds bloom); the coloured speckles on its pavement are gone all the same.

Moving camera, through the v4 denoiser in PyTorch (`stability.py`, 180 frames, moving
objects): the slow change of the lighting ("slow": against its running mean along the
motion; "typical": its median block).

| scene | slow before -> now | typical before -> now |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sponza | 0.0125 -> 0.0099 | 0.0099 -> 0.0075 (-24%) |
| day Bistro | 0.0203 -> 0.0158 | 0.0182 -> 0.0138 (-24%) |
| living room | 0.0088 -> 0.0079 | 0.0045 -> 0.0037 (-18%) |
| night Bistro | 0.0167 -> 0.0166 | 0.0116 -> 0.0114 |

Night Bistro does not move: what changes there under camera motion sits around the lamps
and along the moving objects' paths, not on the surfaces (the map `stability.py --maps`
writes shows it). With a still camera its "slow" is half as large (0.0087).

Cost: the GI pass about doubles. Steam Deck, 1280x800, first version of the fix (3
neighbours, one visibility ray): living room Low 18.9 -> 21.6 ms, Medium 32.5 -> 37.4,
night Bistro Low 54.1 -> 61.2, Medium 94.9 -> 108.0 (+13 to +15%). The final version
reads one more value per neighbour and retries failed neighbours; on the laptop
(640x400) its GI pass takes 4.8 ms against 2.2 without reuse and 3.9 for the first
version. Not yet timed on the Deck.

### What the brightness loss is

A neighbour's reservoir never holds light that neighbour cannot see, but its history
length counts in full, so light a pixel sees and its neighbours do not is under-counted.
Measured in the living room, as shares of the image's brightness: neighbours propose
5.8% that the pixel cannot see (the visibility ray removes it), showing the pixel's own
sample in those cases returns 3.9%, and 1.7% is lost regardless of visibility
(near-field light, whose reconnection is rejected). Requiring the neighbour to face the
same way removed most of it: without that test neighbours on other surfaces in the same
plane (window frames, mouldings) took part, and the loss was 3.6-5%.

Leaving the visibility ray out keeps the brightness (1.00) and costs half as much, but a
long average is further from the reference at fine scale (day Bistro, 4x4 blocks: 0.0169
against 0.0134 with the ray): light leaks into corners.

## A moving view darkened

Found while looking for trails at silhouettes: raw 1-spp frames after 120 frames of a
turning, moving camera (0.4 degrees and a few centimetres per frame), mean of 4-8 runs
with different random sequences, against plain path tracing from the same poses.

| scene | before | unusable samples dropped | and neighbours' fresh samples while moving |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sponza | 0.77 | 0.91 | 0.95 |
| day Bistro | 0.89 | | 0.98 |
| living room | 0.91 | | 0.94 |
| Sponza, slow turn (0.05 degrees per frame) | 0.885 | 0.955 | 0.960 |

Without GI reuse the brightness is 1.00 in all of them, so the loss was ReSTIR GI's.
With one fresh sample per pixel every frame Sponza was at 0.90, with a history of 4
samples at 0.92: the less the history counted, the smaller the loss.

- Cause: when the pixel moves over the surface (or its jittered sample lands on another
  texel of a normal map), the remembered sample can fall below the new point's
  horizon. Its target there is 0, so it was kept with weight 0 and its full history
  length: twenty samples "that found nothing", which dilute every fresh sample by 21,
  and a weight of 0 never recovers. `GI_DROP_UNUSABLE` already dropped hidden picks and
  neighbours outside the Jacobian's range; it now drops these too.
- What remains: every step to another point loses the light that point sees and the
  sample's first owner could not sample. Only fresh samples bring it back, so a pixel
  that moves more than a quarter pixel per frame (by the G-buffer's motion, which does
  not count jitter) also merges its neighbours' fresh samples, as newly visible pixels
  already did. Cost while moving: GI pass 7.2 -> 8.7 ms on the laptop (640x400).
- A still, jittered view lost light the same way. App pipeline, day Bistro, 300 frames
  still: brightness 0.94 -> 0.99 of the reference, PSNR 27.24 -> 27.57 dB; living room
  0.93 -> 0.94, 26.29 -> 26.49 dB. This is the "temporal darkening" noted on 2026-11-18.
- Not solved: the living room keeps 6% under fast motion, and the motion-compensated
  flicker of day Bistro under motion rose from 0.0054 to 0.0059 (more fresh samples).

## Sharper output: the detail upscaler

`--detail-upscale` (launcher: "Detail upscaling") replaces TAAU with the fixed pass the
joint network's output stage was built from: the denoised image is demodulated, gathered
per output pixel, remodulated with output-resolution albedo and accumulated in final
colour. Two faults kept it from being usable and are fixed: directly visible emission
went through the demodulation (a window frame beside a bright window was scaled by the
ratio of the two albedos, up to 90 times: living room 14% too bright, PSNR 19.2 dB), and
it demodulated with a floor of 0.01 whatever the denoiser used. Light seen through glass
or in a mirror is not albedo times light either, so the albedo change from a sample to
the output pixel is now limited to a factor of 3 (+0.1 to +0.4 dB).

Half resolution, today's renderer, round 1 `td3-half16` (see below): TAAU against the
detail upscaler. Still: 300 frames; moving: 200 frames.

| scene | still: PSNR | still: flicker | moving: flicker |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| living room | 26.70 -> 27.09 dB | 0.0015 -> 0.0006 | 0.0021 -> 0.0016 |
| day Bistro | 27.55 -> 28.50 dB | 0.0036 -> 0.0030 | 0.0056 -> 0.0036 |
| Sponza | 30.34 -> 31.39 dB | 0.0013 -> 0.0011 | 0.0017 -> 0.0009 |
| night Bistro | 19.16 -> 19.42 dB | 0.0027 -> 0.0017 | 0.0047 -> 0.0028 |

- Cost on the laptop at 1280x800 with the tuner's visibility raster: +6 ms of 35 (output
  guides 2.6 ms, the pass itself 5.0 ms against TAAU's 1.3). Without the raster the
  output guides are traced and take 7-8 ms.
- At 67% resolution TAAU still has the higher PSNR in the living room (27.43 dB), at a
  higher cost.
- Known fault: under fast motion (0.4 degrees per frame) thin objects and foliage leave
  dark fringes. They are in this frame's gather already, not in the history.
- The joint network's learned head (clip width, blend, confidence, sharpening: 0.2-0.5
  dB in training) is not in the renderer.

## The second-generation denoiser in the renderer

`training/temporal/export.py` now generates every option of `model.py` except the
head's hidden layer: edge stops, history length, the blend as history, the demodulation
floor, exposure-normalized features (a model with those gets a `csTdExposure` kernel
that keeps the displayed frame's mean luminance for the next frame). All options on
match PyTorch to 0.0004 in tonemapped units, in f32 and f16, the same as the old models
(`parity.py`, random weights). The edge stops read two guide values per neighbour: on
the laptop the denoiser takes 4.5 ms against 2.7 ms at 640x400.

Laptop (Iris Xe), living room, 1280x800 at half resolution, with the tuner:

| pipeline | frame | GI pass | denoiser | upscale |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| yesterday, `td-half16`, TAAU | 28.2 ms | 1.9 | 2.7 | 1.3 |
| today, `td-half16`, TAAU | 30.8 ms | 4.5 | 2.6 | 1.3 |
| today, `td3-half16`, TAAU | about 33 ms | 4.5-5.0 | 4.5 | 1.3 |
| today, `td3-half16`, detail upscaler | about 41 ms | 4.5-5.4 | 4.5-4.9 | 5.3 + 2.9 for output guides |

(The laptop's timings move by 10% between runs; the passes are steadier than the sum.)

## Networks on the new inputs

The v4 references with inputs recaptured from the same cameras, windows and moving
objects (the capture is seeded; checked per frame by the crop windows and depth, which
differ only on hair-thin geometry). Raw 1-spp validation L1 against the references:
0.1072 (v4 inputs), 0.0934 (shading reuse), 0.0898 (and the motion fix).

Round 1 (3.6 h on the PC, inputs before the motion fix): `td-half16` and `td2-half24`
fine-tuned from their v4 weights, `td3-half16` (edge stops, history length, blend as
history, floor 0.05, exposure-normalized) from scratch, the joint network from half
time on top of `td3`. Against the references on the recaptured evaluation set (final
renderer, 9 scenes, 2 moving sequences of 8 frames each, 960x540):

| denoiser | PSNR | flicker |
|---|---:|---:|
| v4 td-half16 | 23.75 dB | 0.0956 |
| v4 td2-half24 | 23.86 dB | 0.0952 |
| round 1 td-half16 | 24.00 dB | 0.0953 |
| round 1 td2-half24 | 23.96 dB | 0.0962 |
| round 1 td3-half16 | 24.20 dB | 0.0825 |
| round 2 td-half16 | 23.99 dB | 0.0950 |
| round 2 td2-half24 | 23.98 dB | 0.0949 |
| round 2 td3-half16 | 24.17 dB | 0.0822 |

Round 2: 0.85 h more, each network from its round 1 weights, on inputs recaptured after
the motion fix (`training/runs/v4r/v4r2-*` on the laptop; these are the ones to use,
their inputs match the renderer). It changes the evaluation by less than its noise; on
the held-out scene the L1 fell from 0.0231 to 0.0223 (`td-half16`) and from 0.0133 to
0.0129 (`td3-half16`).

- On their own evaluation set (v4 inputs) the v4 networks scored 23.92 and 23.98 dB with
  flicker 0.0905 and 0.0898: on these short sequences the new inputs are slightly
  harder for them. Shading reuse trades a frame's noise (per pixel +6% in the living
  room, +25% in day Bistro) for independence between frames, which pays off over more
  frames than eight.
- The new architecture is the better denoiser (+0.45 dB over v4 `td-half16`, 14% less
  flicker) and now runs in the renderer. In the app's pipeline it changes little with
  TAAU; its gain shows with the detail upscaler.
- Joint network (exposure-normalized log space, half resolution): round 1 22.53 dB
  against 22.04 dB for `td3` + TAAU, round 2 22.73 against 22.26 dB. Its learned head is
  worth 0.6-0.7 dB in training (round 2 validation 26.81 against 26.20 dB without it);
  held-out scene 23.35 -> 23.87 dB from round 1 to round 2.
- Moving camera (`stability.py`, the sequences above), today's renderer with round 1
  `td3-half16` against yesterday's with v4 `td-half16`: slow change day Bistro 0.0204 ->
  0.0159, night Bistro 0.0167 -> 0.0123, living room 0.0088 -> 0.0072.

## What did not work

- Reusing neighbours' fresh samples into the history every frame
  (`RT_GI_SPATIAL_BELOW=1e9`): noisier per block and 3-4% off in brightness.
- Feeding the pooled result back as the pixel's history: the weights grow without bound
  (brightness x400 and more within 300 frames).
- Limiting a candidate to a multiple of what the others found: -4 to -6% brightness in
  the living room, whose bounce light really does come from rare bright samples.
- A longer history alone (cap 60): no change through the denoiser with a moving camera.
- Lamp reuse settings (cap, neighbours, radius, candidates, every pixel): at most 7% on
  night Bistro; no spatial reuse is 55% worse.
- Path tracing smooth surfaces without reservoirs: no visible change on the living
  room's picture glass.
- Larger training windows and wider filters in the denoiser: see
  `2026-11-19-training-diagnosis.md`.
